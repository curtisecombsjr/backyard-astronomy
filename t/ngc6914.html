NGC 6914 is a cloud of dust about 15 light-years across, 3,000 light-years away in Cygnus. A small group of young stars formed inside it. They are about three times hotter than the Sun, too cool to make the surrounding gas glow. Instead their light bounces off the dust, which scatters blue light best, much as the daytime sky does. Thicker dust shows as dark lanes against red-glowing hydrogen farther back.
