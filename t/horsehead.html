Horsehead Nebula

Barnard 33 · IC 434
  • In the constellation Orion · 1,300 light-years from Earth
  • 10.9 hours · 131 × 300 s · 7 nights · Oct 15 – Nov 12, 2026
  • Askar 120 · ASI2600MC Pro + ASI2600MM Pro · L-Ultimate Hα/OIII

The Horsehead is a pillar of cold, dense gas and dust about 1,300 light-years away. It juts from the edge of a giant dark cloud, which fills the lower half of this picture. The hot star Sigma Orionis (not pictured here) shines on that edge. Its ultraviolet light strips electrons from the hydrogen, which makes it glow. That glowing sheet is IC 434. The Horsehead is too thick for the light to pass through, so it stands in silhouette.
