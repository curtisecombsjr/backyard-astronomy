Tulip Nebula

Sh2-101
  • In the constellation Cygnus · 7,000 light-years from Earth
  • 2.8 hours · 33 × 300 s · 2 nights · Oct 23 – Nov 8, 2026
  • Askar 120 · ASI2600MC Pro + ASI2600MM Pro · L-Ultimate Hα/OIII
  • Annotated1080p 1440p
  • Framed and titled1080p 1440p

The Tulip Nebula is a cloud of glowing hydrogen about 30 light-years across. It glows because of one hot young star near its centre, HDE 227018. The star's ultraviolet light strips electrons from the hydrogen, and the gas shines as they recombine. Above it in this picture is Cygnus X-1, a black hole feeding on a giant star. The black hole's unseen jet has blown a glowing ring in the gas nearby.
