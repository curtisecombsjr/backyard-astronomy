Cosmic Question Mark

Sh2-171 · NGC 7822 · Ced 214 · Berkeley 59
  • In the constellation Cepheus · 3,000 light-years from Earth
  • 0.8 hours · 9 × 300 s · 2 nights · Oct 23 – 24, 2026
  • Askar 120 · ASI2600MC Pro · L-Ultimate Hα/OIII

The Cosmic Question Mark is a cloud of glowing hydrogen about 3,000 light-years away. About 2 million years ago, the star cluster Berkeley 59 formed inside it. Its hottest stars give off ultraviolet light that strips electrons from the hydrogen, which makes it glow. Winds from those stars push the gas outward at up to 12 km/s. Denser clumps resist and are left behind as dark pillars, their tips eroding in the light.
