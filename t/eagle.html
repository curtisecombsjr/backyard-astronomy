Eagle Nebula

M16 · NGC 6611 · IC 4703 · Sh2-49
  • In the constellation Serpens · 5,700 light-years from Earth
  • 12.4 hours · 149 × 300 s · 11 nights · Oct 11 – 23, 2026
  • Askar 120 · ASI2600MC Pro · L-Ultimate Hα/OIII
  • Annotated|1080p|1440p
  • Framed and titled|1080p|1440p

The Eagle Nebula is a cloud of hydrogen lit from inside by a cluster of young stars. The cluster formed about two million years ago. Its 19 biggest stars are each at least 15 times the Sun's mass. Their ultraviolet light strips electrons from the gas, which makes it glow. Nearest the stars, oxygen glows blue; farther out, hydrogen glows gold. The same light eats the cloud away, and its densest parts, the Pillars, erode slowest.
